🌿 The Herbivore Intake Funnel

This station models the selective intake mechanism of an herbivorous digestive system. Herbivores possess highly specialized dentition and gastrointestinal anatomy that are specifically optimized for plant-based diets. Non-plant materials are mechanically incompatible and trigger a rejection or jam response.

Biological Background — Herbivore Adaptations

Dentition: Herbivores possess hypsodont (high-crowned) molars and selenodont teeth with broad, ridged surfaces ideal for lateral grinding of cellulose-rich plant material. Carnassial teeth (for shearing flesh) are absent.

GI Length: Herbivore intestines are proportionally 10–20 times body length (vs. ~5× in carnivores), allowing adequate fermentation time for cellulose breakdown by symbiotic microflora. Ruminants have additional stomach chambers (reticulum, rumen, omasum, abomasum) for regurgitation and re-digestion.

Salivary Chemistry: Herbivore saliva has higher volume and contains buffering bicarbonate to manage fermentation acids. Some species (e.g., horses) can produce up to 35 L of saliva per day.
