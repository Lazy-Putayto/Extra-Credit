⬆ The Nutrient Divergence Point

This station models the critical bifurcation in nutrient absorption pathways at the intestinal villi. Lipids take a unique route — they cannot dissolve directly in blood and must be packaged as chylomicrons before entering the lymphatic system via lacteals. All other nutrients (amino acids, monosaccharides) travel directly into the portal blood capillaries.

Biological Background — Why Lipids Take the Lymphatic Route

Lipid Insolubility: Triglycerides, cholesterol, and fat-soluble vitamins are highly hydrophobic. They cannot dissolve in aqueous portal blood. After absorption by enterocytes, lipids are reassembled into triglycerides and packaged with phospholipids, cholesterol, and apolipoprotein B-48 into chylomicrons.

Lacteal & Lymph: Chylomicrons are too large to enter blood capillaries directly. They are secreted by exocytosis into the central lacteal — a specialized lymph vessel running through each villus. They travel through mesenteric lymphatics and the thoracic duct, finally entering the bloodstream at the left subclavian vein — bypassing the liver initially.

Blood Capillary Route: Amino acids, monosaccharides (glucose, galactose, fructose), and water-soluble vitamins are absorbed via specific carriers (SGLT1, GLUT2, PEPT1, etc.) into the enterocyte and then diffuse/transport into the blood capillaries, entering the hepatic portal vein for direct liver processing.
