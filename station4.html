⚒ The Pepsin pH Gate

This station simulates the pH-dependent activation and deactivation of pepsin, the primary proteolytic enzyme of the stomach. Pepsin functions only within a narrow acidic window and is irreversibly denatured when the pH rises above ~4 as food passes into the small intestine.

Biological Background — Pepsin & Gastric Acid

HCl Secretion: Parietal cells in the gastric fundus secrete hydrochloric acid via the H⁺/K⁺-ATPase (proton pump). This lowers gastric pH to 1.5–3.5, creating the optimal environment for peptic digestion.

Pepsinogen Activation: Chief cells secrete inactive pepsinogen (zymogen). At pH < 5, pepsinogen undergoes autocatalytic activation to pepsin by cleavage of the N-terminal activation peptide. Pepsin is a member of the aspartyl protease family and cleaves peptide bonds adjacent to aromatic amino acids (Phe, Tyr, Trp).

Pancreatic Neutralization: When acidic chyme enters the duodenum, S-cells release secretin, which stimulates pancreatic ductal cells to secrete a bicarbonate-rich fluid. This neutralizes gastric acid, raising duodenal pH to ~7, at which point pepsin is irreversibly denatured. Trypsin, chymotrypsin, and elastase then take over protein digestion.

Pepsin Activity Window

Pepsin has its optimal activity at pH 1.5–2.5. It retains some activity up to pH 4. Above pH 4, pepsin activity drops sharply, and above pH 6 it undergoes irreversible denaturation — meaning it cannot be reactivated even if pH is lowered again. This is in contrast to the reversible inhibition of many other enzymes.
